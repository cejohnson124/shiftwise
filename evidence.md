# Evidence
My old manager would spend 3-5 extra hours creating a schedule for the next week, as well as talking with other managers and taking away from their time as well
## The problem is real
costs time from a managers workload, a bit of money, ~$100-250 from the opportunity cost of making a schedule, and stress from trying to manage too many peoples different schedules
### What research says
**How common it is / what it costs managers**
- **About half of managers spend 3+ hours a week on scheduling.** Legion's 2026 survey of 846 managers and 1,044 hourly employees across 11 industries: "The share of managers who spend three or more hours per week on scheduling declined from 58.5% to 51%." The same survey found 49% of managers still fill open shifts by calling or texting employees one at a time. ([DC Velocity, Sep 2026](https://www.dcvelocity.com/technology/warehouse-it/labor-management/survey-ai-powered-shift-scheduling-tool-can-help-both-employees-and-managers))
- **Matching availability is the hard part.** In Legion's 2021 survey of thousands of North American hourly workers and managers, 48% of managers "struggled daily to match employee preferences and availability with the needs of the business when creating schedules." 45% called this the hardest part of building a schedule, and 35% reported spending 3-10+ hours a week creating and managing schedules. ([Legion, "Hour by Hour" survey report, 2021](https://pages.legion.co/rs/325-EUS-675/images/attracting-retaining-hourly-employees-and-managers-survey.pdf))
- **Schedule changes eat time too.** Same 2021 report: 35% of managers said handling call-outs and no-shows takes the most time and effort, and 73% of employees report unexpected changes by calling or texting their manager. ([Legion 2021](https://pages.legion.co/rs/325-EUS-675/images/attracting-retaining-hourly-employees-and-managers-survey.pdf))
- **Many still do it by hand.** UK retail: "Sixty-five percent of retail managers reported spending more than three hours each week on scheduling," and "Forty-two percent of managers still rely on paper or Excel spreadsheets for scheduling." ([HRreview, Apr 2025, reporting Legion's 2025 UK Hourly Worker report](https://hrreview.co.uk/?p=381815))
- **Restaurants:** "The average restaurant, bar, or club manager spends 2.64 hours, every week, making an employee schedule." Restaurant managers using spreadsheets average 3.14 hours. This was a small local survey and the sample size isn't given. ([TimeForge](https://timeforge.com/blog/restaurant-scheduling-statistics-1/))

**What bad scheduling costs employees** (the result of a hard process)
- In the Harvard/UC Berkeley Shift Project's Michigan survey of service-sector workers, only 41% get 2+ weeks' notice of their schedule and 31% get less than a week. 63% had a last-minute change to a shift's timing or length, and 67% want a more stable, predictable schedule. ([Shift Project, Michigan brief, 2023](https://shift.hks.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/MI_Brief_2022_Update_Layout_Final.pdf))

**Caveat:** Most manager time figures come from companies that sell scheduling software (Legion, TimeForge), so they may overstate the problem. The Shift Project is independent academic research, but it measures what workers experience, not manager time.

## The app helps
Who tests it: several classmates, in a mock scenario.
- 8 fake employees. Each classmate enters 1 conflict for an employee.
- One classmate enters a conflict **after** the deadline, to test late requests.
- The test leaves out the shift-lead and driver/insider rules, since the app isn't meant only for pizza restaurants.
- I play the manager: I run the schedule, review it and approve it.

What I write down:
- How long each classmate takes to enter their conflict. This is something I observe, not a pass/fail limit, since people get as much time as they need before the deadline.
- Any questions they ask or problems they run into while entering it.

What would tell me I am wrong (the app fails if any of these happen):
- Someone is scheduled during a conflict they entered on time.
- Any of the 8 employees doesn't appear on the schedule at least once.
- The late conflict isn't flagged for the manager to handle themselves.
